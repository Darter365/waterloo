# The question (written before play, 12 November 2026)

**From Napoleon's view alone, is there enough to form a plan at 11:00, and to know, by the time it still matters, whether that
plan is working?**

What would count as "enough": the player can say where he believes the enemy's weight is and why; he can say what each of his
orders is meant to do and when he expects to hear of it; and when the picture changes (an attack fails, a new enemy appears) he
learns of it while an order could still answer it.

What we expect to see (stated before the run, so the result can disagree): the opening picture is enough to choose a point of
attack; the weak place is the hour or two after the first orders, when reports are old and the order list says little; the news
of the Prussians arrives with time to act on it.

Settings: Waterloo, Napoleon's view (`--view chief`), seed 1815 (not one of the calibration seeds, 1000 to 1499), history
"uncertain", the 11:00 start, Soult's staff, 45 minutes' silence. One battle. One battle shows what can happen, not how often.

Not asked: whether the game is fun, whether the result is historically right, whether the engine is balanced.

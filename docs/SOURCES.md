# Sources: how well the game's sourced numbers are sourced

A source pass made by Claude on 12 November 2026 (`docs/METHOD.md`, section 2). It lists every number that `docs/RULES.md`,
the research brief and the battle and era packs present as coming from the record, names the source each rests on today,
and sets beside it what a primary document or a standard history says. **It changes no value.** Any change to a value, a
target or a band is a separate task for Chris. The proposals are at the end.

## How to read this

- **Opened** means a research agent fetched the page in this session and read the figure off it. **Recalled** means the
  work is known to say it but was not opened; nothing recalled is offered as evidence. **None found** means what it says.
- **A caution on "opened".** The pages were read through the web fetch tool, which hands back a small model's reading of a
  100,000-character slice of the text, often of a noisy scan. Every figure below was reported from an opened page, but none
  was checked by eye against the page image. **Check a figure against the scan before it goes into a pack or a comment.**
  For the same reason this file gives **no page numbers**: the agents' notes have them as the tool read them, and they are
  not yet trustworthy. Chapters and appendices are named where that helps.
- **Grade** is the packs' own word for how the number is sourced *in the game today*: `doc` (from a document or survey
  someone can open), `approx` (sized from a secondary or web source), `low` (an interpretation of the record), `none`
  (no source). It is not a grade of the better source.
- **Outcome**: `improved` (an openable primary or standard work now covers the number), `part` (it covers some of it),
  `none` (nothing better was found; the row says so).
- "Derived" marks arithmetic done here; the working is shown or is in the row.
- Numbers the rules doc already marks `(est.)` are out of scope, except where a source turned up that bears on one.

## Proposals for Chris

### Values that look wrong

1. **The wind has no source** (F15). "Decision 6 has a source" in the rules doc rests on a 1908 popular history quoting a

2. **An order to Grouchy takes too little time** (C20, C21). The brief has the 10:00 letter arriving "around 13:00"; every
   work opened says 15:30 to 16:00. Both real letters took three and a half to six hours; the game's base is three.
3. **The fire in Wavre** (C9). "Most likely 1 hour" against two hours or more in Houssaye and Kelly. This feeds Bülow's
   arrival, so it is a results-changing question.
4. **Zieten arrives as a whole corps** (A19). The pack brings 23,400 foot and horse; Siborne has only the advance guard in
   action and a corps loss of about 320. Pirch's is similar (A18). No opened work gives either corps' strength on the 18th.
5. **Bülow's corps** (A16, A17). 32,850 foot and horse is above every figure for the whole corps (29,000 to 31,000 with
   gunners), and 64 guns is his first line only; the corps had 86 to 88.
6. **The British contingent** (A12) is about 4,800 over Siborne's foot and horse, and the allied total is about 7,000 over.
   The tab says its strengths are campaign-start figures; that would explain it. The allied guns are 142 against 156 (A13).
7. **French guns: 250 fielded** against 240 to 248 in the histories (A2). Domon and Subervie about 3,000 against 2,000 to
   2,300 (A10). Grouchy's guns 80 against 96 (A20).
8. **Ligny** (D3, D16). Prussian guns 216 against 224 everywhere; Prussian horse 9,870 against 8,150 to 8,800; Henckel 14%
   under Siborne. And Billard was not killed: the pack's note should say he was hurt.
9. **"15,000 left the colours"** (B4): Houssaye has 8,000 to 10,000. **The Hougoumont sentence** (A24): none of its four
   numbers was found anywhere.
10. **Column length** (E6). Clausewitz's hour of road is for 8,000 men, not 6,000, and the rules doc (3.5 km) and the era
    pack (3.0 km) disagree with each other.
11. **Cavalry charge at 400 m a minute** (E7) is above every figure opened (300 to 366). It is marked `(est.)`, so this is
    a note, not an error.
12. **Small ones.** Civil dusk is 21:01, not 21:10 (C23; the era pack is already right). Le Caillou and Rossomme are 2.5
    and 1.3 km back on the game's own map (C24). Pirch was through Wavre *by* 16:00, not after (C12). Vivian's ten yards

    less prisoners) and 25,000 to 27,000 (Houssaye). The band's low end is defensible; its origin, a total read as killed
    and wounded, is not. The band is yours: I am only saying what it rests on.
14. **La Haye Sainte, "about 18:30"** (C14). No witness gives a clock hour. Show "about 18:00 to 18:30", and say that
    an earlier hour is argued.
15. **The Guard's attack** 19:00 to 20:00; **the general advance** one range instead of 19:30 in one rule and 20:00 in
    the brief; **first shots** 11:30 to 11:35 with Wellington's 10:00 noted; **Zieten** 18:00 to 19:30 (C4, C15 to C17).
16. **The rain** 06:00 to 09:00, as one stated range in place of two values in two places (C1).
17. **Ligny's losses** are two camps, not one spread: French 6,800 to 8,500 against 11,000 to 12,000; Prussian about
    12,000 against 15,000 to 18,000. The proposed targets cover both; "most about 16,000" and "25,000" should go (D5, D6).
18. **Quick step by army** (E2): French 65, British 82 m a minute by regulation. One rate for all is a design choice
    worth stating as one; it decides how fast a British line counter-attacks.
19. **The musketry anchors** (F1 to F4). Vitoria is real but is not a 300 m figure; Albuera's 5% could not be traced at
    all. The hit table is honestly "est., calibrated"; its "Anchor" column claims more than the sources give.

### Sources to buy, borrow or open by hand

20. **Open in a browser** (they refuse automated reading or need the page image): Pierre de Wit's waterloo-campaign.nl,
    for Prussian and Netherlands strengths on the day with archive references; Baring's narrative in Beamish's appendix, for
    the sixty rounds (F10); Ropes on the hour La Haye Sainte fell (C14); the return in Dispatches vol. 12 (B5).
21. **A Dutch-Belgian source**, which this pass lacks entirely: De Bas and T'Serclaes de Wommersom, *La campagne de 1815
    aux Pays-Bas* (vol. 3 has the returns); Muilwijk's volumes.
22. **Prussian tables**: Lettow-Vorbeck's appendices (the scan is online; the tables were not reached), Plotho, and
    Wagner's *Plane der Schlachten*; Hofschröer's two volumes for brigades on the day.
23. **French returns**: Couderc de Saint-Chamant, *Napoléon, ses dernières armées*, for regiments on 10 June (the likely
    origin of the Ligny brigades, D2) and the Guard's battalions (A8); Martinien's *Tableaux*.
24. **The two weather papers**: Neumann, *Bulletin of the American Meteorological Society* (1993); Wheeler and Demarée,
    *Weather* 60 (2005).
25. **Modern works the brief quotes second-hand**: Adkin, *The Waterloo Companion* (most useful single book: strengths,
    losses, batteries, Hougoumont); Hughes, *Firepower*; Barbero, *The Battle*; Nafziger, *Imperial Bayonets* (paces,
    file widths, times to change formation); Nosworthy; Muir.
26. **Regulations found but not read**: the Prussian *Exerzir-Reglement* of 1812, Dundas's 1792 *Rules and Regulations*,
    the French cavalry *Ordonnance* of an XIII, Gassendi's *Aide-mémoire*, a later Adye (1813) for the 9-pounder.

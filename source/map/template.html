    About the data

    Ground. Heights are EU-DEM 25 m (Copernicus), read through OpenTopoData as 48 north–south transects of 100 points: one every 0.003° of longitude (about 212 m apart), with points about 79 m apart along each. They are interpolated bicubically to a 50 m grid of 199 × 156 nodes covering 9.9 × 7.75 km.

    Checks. Against a separate 100-point transect down the Brussels road the grid is within 0.8 m RMS (worst 2.4 m). Spot heights: La Belle Alliance 133.5 m against 134.0 m, Mont-Saint-Jean farm 124.4 against 123.9, Rossomme 138.3 against 139.4, Le Caillou 134.0 against 136.4.

    Known distortions. EU-DEM is a surface model, so today’s woods and buildings can add up to about 20 m where they stand. The Lion’s Mound (1823–26) has been removed. The ridge beside it was dug away to build the mound, and today’s ground there may be lower than in 1815; how much lower isn’t recorded in my sources. Modern road cuttings and embankments may move the ground by a few metres. Features narrower than about 200 m east–west (sunken lanes, banks, the sandpit) are too small for the grid and are drawn as symbols.

    Relief. Height is shown three ways. A watercolour tint changes every 5 m, from sage in the Lasne valley to terracotta on the southern plateau. Contours run every 5 m and are numbered every 10 m. Lehmann hachures (1799) run straight downhill, and their ink weight shows the slope. Lehmann’s scale reaches solid black at 45°; this sheet reaches it at 12°, because nothing here is steeper than about 15° and at Lehmann’s scale the main ridges would be almost blank.

    Smoothing. For drawing only, the heights are smoothed over about 80 m. That hides the small bumps, which are mostly modern trees, buildings and data noise rather than 1815 ground. The sight-line and dead-ground tools and the height readout still use the unsmoothed grid, so the readout can differ from the tint band by a metre or two.

    Water. The brooks are worked out from the terrain itself: every stream draining more than 0.75 km² is drawn. They were not surveyed, but they land where the Smohain and the Lasne actually run.

    Features. The Brussels chaussée is drawn through surveyed points (Wikipedia’s list of battlefield locations). Farms are placed at surveyed coordinates. Other roads, the woods, hedges and enclosures are traced from memory of period plans and are good to about 100–200 m. Village houses are symbolic. The edge of the Forêt de Soignes in 1815 is uncertain and is not drawn.

    Positions. Brigade strengths come from the order of battle in the rules doc, with II Corps reduced for Quatre Bras and the Young Guard for Ligny. French II Corps is cut proportionally, by 21.5%. Allied brigades are at campaign strength, less the detachments shown in the farms. Placement follows the standard accounts (Siborne, Adkin and others). The French I Corps line follows the lane from La Belle Alliance toward La Haye, 1,000–1,200 m from the allied crest, which agrees with Project Hougoumont’s reading. The uncertainty halos show how far I would trust each position.
